Drive to the kitchen.

A worked example of RFC-0268's commercial-use gate. This manifest is a
production deployment (`deployment.commercial_use: true`) that declares NLLB-200
translation weights, which are CC-BY-NC (non-commercial). RFC-0262 records the
component's `commercial_use_gate`; RFC-0268 supplies the other half, whether the
deployment itself is commercial, and closes the loop.

Run it under the default policy:

  urml validate commercial-gate.urml.yaml \
    -m commercial-gate.manifest.yaml --profile home

Expected: REJECTED with policy.commercial_use_gate_violated. A commercial
deployment may not ship a non-commercial component.

Now run the same with --no-policy:

  urml execute commercial-gate.urml.yaml \
    -m commercial-gate.manifest.yaml --profile home --no-policy

It validates (with a soft advisory) and executes. And if the deployment were
research rather than production, flip `commercial_use` to false: the gate is
satisfied and it passes under the default policy too. The license names the
constraint; the deployment posture decides whether it bites.
