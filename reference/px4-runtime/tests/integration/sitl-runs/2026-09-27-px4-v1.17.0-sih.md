# PX4 SITL run, 2026-09-27: PX4 v1.17.0, SIH quadrotor

**Result: the vehicle flew the `drone/flight_only_positive` program.** URML's PX4 adapter armed a simulated PX4 quadrotor, took off to 30 m, flew to a waypoint 15 m north, returned to launch and landed. PX4's own log records the arm, the take-off, the return, the landing and the disarm, and a listen-only witness on PX4's ground-station link measured the flight.

This is a simulated autopilot on a developer machine. It is not a CI run and not a hardware flight.


## What the witness measured

The witness is a second pymavlink connection on UDP 14550, the port PX4 SITL streams its ground-station link to. It only listens, so it cannot steer the flight, and it computes its numbers with its own arithmetic, not the adapter's.

| Check | Required | Measured |
|---|---|---|
| Vehicle armed | yes | yes |
| Highest relative altitude (`GLOBAL_POSITION_INT.relative_alt`) | at least 27.0 m (90 percent of the 30 m take-off) | **31.79 m** |
| Closest approach to the 15 m waypoint | within 3.0 m (twice the 1.5 m acceptance radius) | **0.92 m** |
| Landed state at the end (`EXTENDED_SYS_STATE`) | ON_GROUND | ON_GROUND; the sequence was on ground, taking off, in air, landing, on ground (1, 3, 2, 4, 1) |
| Armed at the end | not required | no (PX4 disarmed itself after landing) |

The highest relative altitude is above 30 m because PX4 v1.17 corrects its home altitude against GNSS in flight, and SIH's simulated GNSS is noisy: `relative_alt` wandered by about a metre around the 30 m hover while PX4's local-frame altitude stayed steady.

## The earlier green that does not count

Earlier the same day (PX4 ULog `07_33_41.ulg`), the same gate test passed in 2.37 s against the same PX4 build, with URML at `bc4c490`. PX4's log from that run shows "Ready for takeoff!", "Returning to launch" and "Landing at current position", and no arming and no take-off. The adapter at `bc4c490` reported every COMMAND_ACK as success, so the fixture passed while the simulated vehicle stayed on the ground. No green result from before this record stands as evidence that the PX4 adapter flies.

## Limits

- Simulation only. SIH is PX4's built-in, simplified physics; no Gazebo, no wind, no sensor faults.
- One program, the flight-only fixture. Perception and manipulation are not part of the PX4 adapter.
- Not CI. The `px4-sitl-e2e` job in `.github/workflows/px4-integration.yml` has not run, and it still boots jMAVSim rather than SIH.
- No hardware. Nothing here says a physical aircraft flew.
